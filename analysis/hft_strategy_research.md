# High-Turnover Strategy Design — openclaw-poly-bot
Small bankroll ($100 paper, ~$2/position), Python, existing 5-min loop, shadow ledger available. All numbers below use the measured 2026-06-12 microstructure snapshot and the live fee formula `fee = C × feeRate × p × (1−p)` (taker-only; Crypto 0.07, Sports 0.03, Politics/Finance 0.04, Geopolitics 0; makers always free).

## 0. What the measurements rule out (do not build these)

- **Taker momentum/latency on crypto up/down markets.** Round-trip taker cost on BTC daily at p≈0.50 is ~1c spread + 2 × 1.75c fee = **~4.5c/share**. Only 17.2% of 30-min windows move ≥5c, and the book tracks Chainlink near-instantly, so capturing that tail is a latency race we lose from a Python REST bot. Structurally unprofitable. Same conclusion, worse, for 5m/15m windows ($0.2k–$25k volume, $0.6–2.6k depth).
- **negRisk / YES+NO arbitrage.** Measured live: 17/18 multi-outcome events had sum-of-asks ≥ 100c (median 101.7c); the one exception offered 0.5c gross on $92 depth. Zero opportunities clear fees. Academic-era profits are arbed away at the ~3s timescale. No scanner.
- **General-market intraday momentum (non-geopolitics).** Only 1.9% of 30-min windows move ≥2c across liquid mid-band markets; politics/finance takers pay 0.04-rate fees on top. Signal starvation + fees.

## 1. Ranked candidates

### #1 — Geopolitics intraday momentum (taker, fee-free) — RECOMMENDED
- **Mechanism:** Geopolitics is the only 0% taker-fee category and the only non-crypto category with measurable intraday movement (Iran-deadline markets: 8.4% of 30-min windows ≥2c vs 1.9% baseline). Detect a ≥2–3c move over a trailing ~30 min in a watchlist of geopolitics markets; buy the direction of the move with FOK/FAK; exit intraday on profit-take, stop, or timeout.
- **Edge net of fees+spread:** Fee = $0 (verify per-market via `feeSchedule`/`getClobMarketInfo`, don't hardcode). Cost = spread only: measured geopolitics books are thin ($1–2k top-5 depth) but tight; assume 0.5–1.0c round trip; slippage ≈ 0 at $2 size against ≥$300 depth. Required edge: average continuation > ~1c after a 2c trigger. **This is the one unmeasured quantity — and it is fully backtestable with prices-history before writing any trading code.** If the event study shows mean net capture ≥ +1.5c/share (≈ +4–8% on a 0.15–0.40 entry), go; if < +0.5c, kill the idea at zero cost.
- **Frequency / holding:** ~48 30-min windows/day × 8% fire rate on hot markets, heavily deduped → realistic 1–3 entries/day across a 15–30 market watchlist. Holding 30 min–4 h; capital recycles same day.
- **Risks:** regime-dependent (current Iran news cycle may be the entire signal — 15-day backtest is non-stationary); thin books mean exits in a panic may slip a few cents; Polymarket can add geopolitics fees at any time (guardrail below); signal is news-driven so it clusters (cap concurrent positions).
- **Infrastructure:** **none new.** The signal lives on a 30-min timescale, so the existing 5-min `run_cycle()` is fast enough — same snapshot-diff pattern as `whale_monitor.py`, but persist the mid-price ring buffer to a state JSON (whale monitor's in-memory `_previous_snapshots` loses history on restart). Shadow's taker-FOK fill simulation is appropriate as-is, and the ≤5-min staleness is testable in backtest (enter one bar late).

### #2 — Late-window calibration maker on BTC hourly/daily up/down
- **Mechanism:** In the final ~10–15 min of an hourly window (or final 1–2 h of the daily), post a post-only GTD bid at 0.90–0.95 on the leading side; resolution arrives within minutes (24 cycles/day on hourly = excellent turnover). Maker ⇒ fee = $0 despite the 0.07 crypto rate.
- **Edge math:** Buy at 0.93: win +7c, lose −93c ⇒ breakeven win rate 93%. If P(win | mid≈0.93 at T−10m) = 0.95, EV ≈ +2.2% per trade, ~24 shots/day. The calibration P(win | price, time-left) **is backtestable** from prices-history (15d × 24 hourly windows = 360 samples). The killer unknown is **fill conditioning**: a resting bid at 0.93 fills disproportionately when price is falling through it (bad news), so realized win rate < unconditional calibration — this cannot be backtested, only shadow-measured with a maker-fill simulator that the repo doesn't have (shadow is taker-only today).
- **Frequency/holding:** up to 24/day, holds of minutes. Risks: fat-tail −93c losses (one loss erases ~13 wins), adverse selection vs takers with live Binance feeds (500ms taker delay removed Feb 2026). Infra: new fast daemon (sub-minute order management), post-only/GTD wiring (library supports it: `post_order(..., post_only=True)`, `OrderArgs.expiration`), maker-fill shadow simulation, file locking on the shared JSON ledgers. **Build second, only after #1 ships and the calibration backtest clears 95%.**

### #3 — Passive spread-capture MM on BTC daily up/down
- **Mechanism:** Quote both sides of the 1c-wide BTC daily book post-only; earn the spread + maker rebates; the market resolves daily (no multi-week holds). This is the best-documented retail-scale strategy class ($200–800/day on $10k, per Polymarket's own writeup) and the microstructure brief's nominal favorite.
- **Honest small-size problem:** the book shows **$11–13k resting per side at a 0.01 tick** — a 1c spread you cannot quote inside of. A $2 order joins the back of an $11k queue and fills mostly when price trades *through* the level, i.e., the fills you get are the adversely-selected ones, in a market where 35% of 30-min windows move ≥2c. Rebates are irrelevant at this bankroll: a $2 fill generates ~$0.035 fee-equivalent; a pro-rata share of 20% of the pool is sub-cent/day and may never clear the $1 pUSD payout minimum. Gross spread capture ~1c/share (~2% of notional) vs adverse-selection cost plausibly ≥ that.
- **Verdict:** right idea at $10k bankroll, wrong at $2 positions. Not backtestable (no historical book/queue data). Park it; revisit if bankroll scales 50–100x.

## 2. Recommendation: #1, Geopolitics Momentum ("GEOMOM")

Justification from the data: it is the **only** strategy where (a) fees are exactly $0 by category, (b) measured spreads (0.5–1c round trip) are small relative to the 2–5c moves that actually occur, (c) the existing 5-min loop is fast enough (30-min signal timescale), (d) the existing shadow taker-fill simulation is honest (signal is not latency-critical, unlike everything crypto), and (e) the core unknown — momentum continuation — is decidable from a free prices-history backtest **before** writing trading code. #2 and #3 both require a new fast daemon, maker-fill simulation, file locking, and accept unbacktestable adverse-selection risk. GEOMOM is testable in shadow within ~2–3 days of work.

## 3. GEOMOM specification

**Universe (refreshed each cycle):** Gamma markets in the geopolitics fee category (confirm `fd.r == 0` via `getClobMarketInfo(conditionID)` — Gamma returns no category field, classify by event tags/keywords then verify fee), `volume24hr ≥ $10k`, binary, mid in 0.05–0.90, resolves > 24 h out (avoid resolution gamma), top-5 min-side depth ≥ $300, best bid-ask spread ≤ 1.5c. Expect 10–30 markets.

**Entry rule (evaluated each 5-min cycle against a persisted ring buffer of per-cycle mids):**
1. `mid_now − mid_30min_ago ≥ +3c` for the YES token (or ≤ −3c, in which case the tradeable side is NO at `1 − p`). Backtest will tune trigger ∈ {2c, 3c, 4c}.
2. Move not retraced: `mid_now ≥ mid_peak_30min − 1c`.
3. Confirmation: signal present on two consecutive cycles (kills one-print noise; backtest quantifies the cost of the ~5-min delay).
4. The token being bought is priced **0.05–0.40**. Two reasons: payoff asymmetry, and the ~$1/5-share minimum order means $2 cannot buy a token above $0.40 (5 shares × 0.40 = $2). Signals whose tradeable side is >0.40 are skipped and logged as `skip_price_band`.
5. Spread at entry ≤ 1.5c; market not on cooldown; all global circuit breakers pass (`check_circuit_breakers`).
- Execute via `execution.execute_strategy_buy` (FOK; switch to FAK later if FOK rejections appear in logs).

**Exit rules (checked every cycle by the strategy itself — do not rely on `run_shadow_check`'s sweep):**
- **Profit-take:** bid ≥ entry + 5c → `execute_sell` (taker, still fee-free).
- **Stop:** bid ≤ entry − 3c → sell.
- **Timeout:** 4 h elapsed → sell at market regardless.
- **Safety:** sell if market enters final 24 h to resolution or spread blows out > 3c with position in profit.
- Asymmetric +5/−3 with a backtested >50% continuation rate is the starting grid; backtest picks the actual TP/SL/timeout from the MFE/MAE distributions.

**Sizing:** flat $2.00/position (`GEOMOM_MAX_POSITION_USD = config.MAX_POSITION_USD`). No pyramiding, no averaging down.

**Guardrails (mirror the Tipoff90/Longshot pattern in config.py):**
```
GEOMOM_MAX_POSITION_USD   = 2.00
GEOMOM_MAX_TRADES_PER_DAY = 4        # global MAX_DAILY_TRADES=6 and MAX_DAILY_LOSS_USD=3.00 still apply
GEOMOM_MAX_OPEN_POSITIONS = 4        # news clusters; cap correlated exposure
GEOMOM_MAX_SPREAD         = 0.015
GEOMOM_COOLDOWN_HOURS     = 2        # per-market re-entry cooldown
GEOMOM_KILL_AFTER_TRADES  = 30       # auto-disable if cumulative ROI < 0 after 30 closed trades
GEOMOM_DISABLED_FILE      = state/GEOMOM_DISABLED
GEOMOM_REQUIRE_ZERO_FEE   = True     # skip any market whose live fd.r != 0 (fees changed 3x Jan–Mar 2026)
```

**Logging (one JSONL record per signal, fired or filtered):** timestamp, market slug/token, trigger move size & window, mid at signal vs actual fill price (quantifies 5-min staleness slippage), spread and min-side depth at entry, fee paid (assert 0), shares, every exit check (MFE/MAE path at cycle granularity), exit reason (tp/sl/timeout/safety), holding time, realized P&L, and skip reasons (`skip_price_band`, `skip_spread`, `skip_depth`, `skip_cooldown`). Cycle-level: watchlist size, signals fired/filtered.

## 4. Validation plan

**Phase 0 — backtest first, build second (1 day, no trading code).** prices-history at fidelity=1 supports it, respecting the 15-day span cap (chunk requests per the API-quirks memory):
- Pull 1-min series for all geopolitics watchlist markets (and recently-resolved ones via Gamma) for the last 15 days.
- Event study on every ≥2c/3c/4c 30-min move: forward return at +30m/+1h/+2h/+4h, MFE/MAE, then simulate the TP/SL/timeout grid with a flat **1c round-trip spread haircut** (historical spreads unavailable; 1c is the conservative end of measured).
- Staleness test: re-run with entry delayed 5 and 10 minutes after the trigger bar. If the edge dies with a 5-min delay, the 5-min loop is insufficient and the strategy needs the fast-poller phase (or is dead).
- **Go/no-go:** mean net capture ≥ +1.5c/share and >0 in at least 2 of 3 five-day sub-windows → build. < +0.5c or sign-flips across sub-windows → stop here, total cost one day.
- Honest limitation: 15 days, one dominant news regime (Iran). Treat the backtest as a *filter*, not proof.

**What backtest cannot show (shadow must):** real fill prices vs FOK book-walk, actual spreads/depth at signal time, fee-category drift, clustering of concurrent signals, restart behavior of the persisted ring buffer.

**Phase 1 — shadow (2–3 weeks).** Run inside the existing loop in shadow mode. Shadow's taker fill (live book walk + real fee curve) is adequate here because the signal is 30-min-scale; for extra honesty, log mid-at-signal vs fill-at-execution each trade — that difference *is* the latency cost, measured for free. Promotion criteria: ≥25 closed trades over ≥10 trading days, cumulative ROI > 0 net, max drawdown < $5, realized fill slippage vs signal mid < 1c median. Auto-disable fires at 30 trades if ROI < 0.

**Phase 2 (only after Phase 1 passes):** 30–60s fast-poller thread (batched `get_prices` is cheap; venv already has `websockets` 15.0.1 for a WS upgrade) to cut staleness — run as a **thread inside main.py**, not a second process, to avoid the unlocked read-modify-write race on `shadow_ledger.json`/`open_orders.json`. In parallel, start the #2 calibration backtest (P(win | price, time-left) on hourly BTC windows) so the next strategy's go/no-go is data-driven too.

**Expected economics, stated plainly:** at $2/trade, 1–3 trades/day, and a backtest-confirmed +4–8% net per trade, this earns cents per day — the purpose at this bankroll is to validate a fee-free, fast-turnover ROI engine in live microstructure, not income. If shadow confirms the per-trade ROI, size is the only knob (geopolitics depth supports ~$200–500 positions before slippage matters, ~100–250x headroom).

# Appendix: Research Briefings

## fees-rebates
Polymarket's current (June 2026) fee structure, verified live from docs.polymarket.com: fees are taker-only, set by the protocol at match time (since CLOB V2 on 2026-04-28 there is no feeRateBps on orders; fee parameters come from the market's feeSchedule / getClobMarketInfo(conditionID) which returns fd = { r: feeRate, e: exponent, to: takerOnly }). The formula is fee = C × feeRate × p × (1 − p), where C = shares and p = price. Per-category feeRates: Crypto 0.07, Sports 0.03, Finance/Politics/Mentions/Tech 0.04, Economics/Culture/Weather/Other 0.05, Geopolitics 0 (fee-free). Makers are never charged. Fees peak in USDC at p=0.50 ($1.75 per 100 shares on crypto, $0.75 sports, $1.00 for 4% categories, $1.25 for 5% categories) and fall symmetrically to ~0 at the extremes; fees round to 5 decimals with a 0.00001 USDC minimum. Since March 1, 2026, taker fees apply to ALL crypto markets — 15-min, 5-min, 1H, 4H, daily, weekly — at the same 0.07 rate (the original Jan 5 launch on 15-min markets peaked at 1.56%, i.e. ~0.0625, raised to 0.07 in the March 30 'Fee Structure V2' that also extended fees to nearly every category).

Two rebate programs exist. (1) Maker Rebates Program: makers pay zero fees AND receive a daily pro-rata share of taker fees — 20% of collected fees in Crypto, 25% in all other fee-enabled categories — distributed per-market by fee-equivalent weight (rebate = your_fee_equivalent / total_fee_equivalent × rebate_pool), paid daily in pUSD with a $1 minimum accrual; eligibility is simply having resting orders that get filled. (2) Taker Rebate Program (live May 28–29, 2026): seven tiers based on 30-day Weighted Volume, wV = Trade Size × (1 − Entry Price) × Category Weight × Bonuses, with category weights Sports 1.0, Politics/Finance/Mentions/Tech 1.3, Econ/Culture/Weather/Other 1.7, Crypto 2.3, Geopolitics 0. Tiers: Bronze $2k wV → 3% rebate ($10 one-time bonus), Silver $20k → 8% ($50), Gold $200k → 18% ($250), Platinum $1M → 32% ($1,500), Diamond $4M → 44% ($7,500), Obsidian $10M+ → 50% ($25,000). Tier recalculates daily at midnight UTC; rebates pay daily in pUSD, forward-looking only (no backfill); wash trading/self-matching voids rebates. There are no volume-based discounts on the headline taker fee itself — the tiered taker rebate is the only volume mechanism — and there is also a separate Liquidity Rewards program for makers quoting two-sided markets.

Implications: For a fast-turnover strategy the per-leg taker fee is feeRate × p × (1−p) per share, so a taker round trip costs roughly 2 × feeRate × p(1−p) per share (slightly less if the exit price moved). Concrete worst cases at p≈0.50: crypto ~3.5% of notional round trip, sports ~1.5%, politics/finance ~2.0%, econ/other ~2.5% — these are enormous relative to typical short-horizon edges, and crypto hourly/daily markets are the MOST expensive category (0.07), not exempt, since March 1, 2026. Three structural mitigations: (1) Trade near the extremes — fee as % of dollars deployed is feeRate × (1−p), so the existing NBA Tipoff90-style entries at ~0.90 in Sports cost only ~0.3% of capital per taker leg, while crypto longshot buys at 0.10 cost 6.3%; (2) Convert legs to maker fills — makers pay zero and additionally collect 20–25% of the market's taker fee pool daily, so a maker-entry/maker-exit version of any strategy is fee-free and yield-positive, at the cost of fill uncertainty; (3) Exploit the Taker Rebate tiers — a bot doing sustained taker flow should size to hold a tier: $20k 30-day wV (Silver) refunds 8% of fees, $200k (Gold) 18%, $1M (Platinum) 32%. Note wV is boosted 2.3x in crypto and by (1−p) for cheap entries, so a high-frequency crypto bot reaches tiers fastest but also bleeds the most gross fees — at Platinum the net crypto rate is still ~0.048 effective. Geopolitics remains the only fully fee-free category but earns no rebates/wV. Practically: pull live fee params per market via the feeSchedule object / getClobMarketInfo (info.fd = {r, e, to}) rather than hardcoding, since rates changed three times Jan–Mar 2026 and Polymarket states they can change without notice; and never wash-trade for wV — it voids rebates and tier status. ## api-capabilities
Polymarket's CLOB API (as documented live on docs.polymarket.com, June 2026) exposes four WebSocket feeds: an unauthenticated market channel at wss://ws-subscriptions-clob.polymarket.com/ws/market (book snapshots, price_change, tick_size_change, last_trade_price, plus opt-in custom_feature_enabled events like best_bid_ask, new_market, market_resolved), an authenticated user channel at .../ws/user (order and trade lifecycle: MATCHED -> MINED -> CONFIRMED, with RETRYING/FAILED) requiring apiKey/secret/passphrase, a sports channel, and an RTDS feed. Market/user channels need a PING every ~10s; the docs publish no latency figures or WS rate limits. The May 2025 changelog removed the old 100-token subscription cap on the market channel.

Order capabilities are now richer than older training data suggests: GTC, GTD (UTC-seconds expiry), FOK, and FAK are all supported, and post-only became available Jan 6, 2026 (GTC/GTD only; combining with FOK/FAK returns INVALID_POST_ONLY_ORDER_TYPE). A HeartBeats API (also Jan 6, 2026) auto-cancels all open orders if a heartbeat session lapses ~10s (plus 5s buffer) — docs recommend sending every 5s with the last heartbeat_id; this is a dead-man's-switch for bots, though docs are ambiguous about whether it is opt-in per session. Tick sizes are per-market (0.1/0.01/0.001/0.0001) and tighten to 0.001 beyond 0.96/0.04, broadcast live via tick_size_change. REST rate limits (raised April 8 and June 1, 2026) are generous: POST /order and DELETE /order at 5,000 req/10s burst and 120,000/10min (200/s) sustained; /book and /price 1,500 req/10s; /prices-history 1,000 req/10s; excess traffic is throttled/queued by Cloudflare, not rejected.

Critically for tooling: the legacy py-clob-client repo is ARCHIVED. An April 17, 2026 changelog entry mandates CLOB V2 migration to py-clob-client-v2 (PyPI v1.0.1, May 9, 2026, supporting GTC/GTD/FOK/FAK and a postOnly flag in PostOrdersArgs), and Polymarket also ships a beta unified SDK (pip install --pre polymarket-client). The legacy v1 client (last release v0.34.6, Feb 19, 2026) still works for now but should not be the basis of new code. None of the Python clients bundle WebSocket support — the WS feeds must be consumed with a separate websocket library.

Implications: For a fast-turnover bot the infrastructure is no longer the bottleneck: 200/s sustained order placement and 1,500 req/10s on /book are far above what an NBA-tipoff-style strategy needs, and throttle-not-reject semantics mean transient bursts degrade to queuing latency rather than errors — but that same queuing means you should never rely on REST polling for time-critical signals. Use the unauthenticated market WS channel (book + price_change + best_bid_ask via custom_feature_enabled) for signal generation and the authenticated user channel for fill confirmation instead of polling GET /order; the MATCHED -> MINED -> CONFIRMED status stream tells you when settlement risk has passed. FAK is the right entry type for taking liquidity with partial-fill tolerance (better than FOK for thin books), while post-only GTC/GTD lets you quote passively without ever paying taker fees — important since 2026 fee changes made taker fees real in several categories (check the market's fee category; sports/geopolitical were still maker-free as of March 2026). Two operational landmines: (1) the heartbeat dead-man's switch — if your bot starts a heartbeat session and then stalls >15s, every resting order is cancelled; this is actually desirable for a fast-turnover bot (free kill-switch) but must be wired into the main loop, and the opt-in-vs-mandatory ambiguity should be tested empirically before deploying resting orders; (2) the codebase currently pins legacy py-clob-client, which is archived and officially deprecated as of April 17, 2026 — migrate to py-clob-client-v2 (or the beta polymarket-client SDK) before building new order logic, and budget for hand-rolling the WS consumer since no official Python package ships one. Finally, handle tick_size_change events: near 0.96/0.04 the tick tightens to 0.001 and stale 0.01-grid quotes will be rejected mid-flight with INVALID_ORDER_MIN_TICK_SIZE.

## strategy-landscape
Short-horizon trading on Polymarket in 2025-2026 is dominated by bots, and the evidence is unusually quantifiable because everything is on-chain. The best-documented edge is structural arbitrage: a peer-reviewed AFT 2025 paper ("Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest", arxiv 2508.03474) measured $39.6M of realized arbitrage profit from April 2024-April 2025, of which ~$23-29M came from negRisk multi-outcome rebalancing (sum of mutually-exclusive YES prices != 1.0), ~$10.6M from single-condition YES+NO mispricing, and only ~$95K from cross-market combinatorial arb. Profits are concentrated: the top wallet made $2.01M over 4,049 transactions and the top 10 took ~$7.8M. However this trade has been heavily competed away — opportunity duration reportedly collapsed from ~12s (2024) to ~3s, requiring sub-100ms execution on dedicated Polygon RPC infrastructure; a viral open-source "YES+NO<$1" arb bot publicly failed because real opportunities are gone before slow takers arrive.

The second well-evidenced edge was latency arbitrage in fast-resolving crypto up/down markets (5/15-minute BTC/ETH/SOL): bots watched Binance/Coinbase spot via websocket and bought the lagging side of Polymarket's 2-10s-stale order book near 50/50 odds. One wallet famously turned ~$313 into ~$414K in a month. Polymarket killed this in January 2026 with a dynamic taker fee (fee = C x 0.25 x (p(1-p))^2, peaking ~1.5-3% near p=0.5 — exactly where the latency trade lived) and in February 2026 removed the 500ms taker delay. The economics consequently flipped to the maker side: taker fees fund daily USDC maker rebates, and post-change commentary (BlockBeats) reports top bots are profitable on rebates alone without capturing spread. Market making + liquidity rewards is the most consistently confirmed "grinder" strategy overall — Polymarket's own Oracle newsletter documents a maker (@defiance_cr, open-sourced bot) earning $200/day on $10K rising to $700-800/day at peak, by targeting low-volatility markets whose reward rate didn't price their risk; returns fell after the 2024 election as the rewards pool shrank.

Momentum/whale-following and news-latency are real but softer-evidence edges. Polymarket's COPYTRADE WARS post documents a copy-trading arms race (Stand.trade, Polymarket's own COPYCAT product, PolyTrack etc.); one follower claims >$10K/month, and whales now obfuscate via multiple wallets, so the signal decays — practitioners advise skipping a copy if price already moved >10% from the whale's entry, and some now counter-trade historically negative-P&L wallets. News/event-latency trading is best documented in esports: bots parsing official League of Legends/Dota 2 game APIs got a 30-40 second information lead and reportedly made >$200K in late 2025; the same logic applies to live sports (courtsiding) and breaking-news spike bots. Finance Magnates reports 14 of the top 20 wallets on Polymarket's profit leaderboard are bots. Caveat: several precise-sounding numbers circulating (win rates like "78-85% for market making", monthly return ranges) come from low-quality, likely AI-generated Medium/SEO content and should be treated as directional at best.

## infra-gap
The bot is a single synchronous daemon (`bot/main.py:main`) that runs `run_cycle()` then sleeps `config.LOOP_INTERVAL_SEC = 300` seconds (config.py:104), sliced into 1-second sleeps only for clean shutdown (main.py:1101-1104). The effective reaction time is cycle-duration + 300s: every cycle sequentially runs the threshold fast-path, Tipoff 90, Longshot, shadow upkeep, order management, the trade queue, position/guardrail checks (one REST `get_book` per position), redemption, gov feeds, whale monitor, and earnings scraping; every 6th cycle (~30 min) it adds news + LLM position analysis, and every 12th (~60 min) full LLM market scans — so a "fast" sub-strategy like whale_monitor.py actually samples books once per 5+ minutes (its `time_delta` is whatever elapsed between cycles). All execution today is REST via api.py: FOK market orders (`market_buy`/`market_sell`, api.py:224-280, using `MarketOrderArgs(order_type=OrderType.FOK)`), and GTC limit orders (`place_limit_buy`/`place_limit_sell`, api.py:192-222, via `create_and_post_order` whose `post_order` default is GTC with `post_only=False`).

The installed py-clob-client 0.34.6 (venv/lib/python3.14/site-packages/py_clob_client/) supports more than the bot uses: `OrderType` enum has GTC, FOK, GTD, FAK (clob_types.py:12-16); `OrderArgs.expiration` (clob_types.py:77) enables GTD; `post_order(order, orderType, post_only)` (client.py:623) supports post-only for GTC/GTD (raises otherwise, client.py:627-628); `post_orders` batch with `PostOrdersArgs(postOnly=...)` (client.py:592, clob_types.py:254-257); plus batch market-data calls useful for fast polling — `get_order_books`, `get_prices`, `get_midpoints`, `get_last_trades_prices` — and an RFQ module (rfq/). The library has NO WebSocket support at all (grep for websocket/wss/subscribe returns nothing); it is pure REST over requests. However the venv already contains `websockets` 15.0.1 and `aiohttp` 3.14.1 (transitive deps, not in requirements.txt), so a client for Polymarket's separate WSS market/user channels could be built without new installs.

For a 10-60s strategy, shrinking LOOP_INTERVAL_SEC is not viable — a cycle does dozens of sequential REST calls and (on verbose cycles) multi-minute LLM batches, and the every-6th/12th-cycle cadence logic assumes 5-minute cycles. The right shape is a separate fast daemon (or a thread inside main.py) that watches a small token set via WebSocket or tight batched REST polling and reuses `execution.execute_strategy_buy`/`execute_sell`. The main blocker is shared JSON state with no locking: `shadow.load_ledger`/`save_ledger` (shadow.py:79-111) and `execution.load_open_orders`/`save_open_orders` do unlocked read-modify-write, so a second process racing the 5-minute loop can lose updates. The shadow layer itself (shadow.py) is callable at any frequency and fills FOK against the live book with real fees, but for fast strategies its fills are optimistic: it fills at the book observed at decision time with zero signal-to-fill latency, no market impact, no partial fills, and no limit/maker simulation — exactly the slippage a 10-60s racing strategy would actually pay.

Implications: A 10-60s reaction strategy cannot live inside run_cycle(): the 300s sleep plus a multi-second-to-multi-minute cycle body makes the floor ~5-10 minutes, and simply lowering LOOP_INTERVAL_SEC would hammer Gamma/CLOB with the full scan stack and break the every-6th/12th-cycle cadence assumptions. The clean design is a separate fast daemon (e.g. bot/fastpath.py run as its own process or a thread started from main()) that (a) watches a pre-selected small token set, via either Polymarket's WSS market channel using the already-installed websockets 15.0.1, or 1-5s batched REST polling with client.get_order_books/get_prices (cheap for tens of tokens), and (b) reuses execution.execute_strategy_buy / execute_sell so logging, journaling, alerts, and the file-based circuit breakers keep working. Required changes: add file locking (or a single-writer rule / per-strategy ledger file) around shadow_ledger.json, open_orders.json and the trade log, because the current read-modify-write is unsafe across processes; if threading is chosen instead, a process-wide lock suffices and in-memory caches (_clob_client, sell cooldowns) are shared for free. Order-type-wise the library already supports what a fast taker strategy needs — FOK is used today, FAK would allow partial-fill takes, and GTD+post_only enables short-lived maker quotes that avoid taker fees entirely — none of which require new dependencies. For paper validation, bot/shadow.py works mechanically at any call frequency (shadow_buy/shadow_sell are plain functions on a JSON ledger), but its fills are systematically optimistic for fast strategies: it fills at the book seen at decision time with zero signal-to-order latency, no competition, no partial fills, and no maker/limit simulation. To trust shadow results for a speed-sensitive edge, add a latency-adjusted fill mode (re-fetch the book after a configurable delay, or fill against the next observed book snapshot) and have the fast daemon manage its own exits rather than relying on run_shadow_check's 5-minute SL/TP sweep. Without that, expect live performance to undershoot the shadow ledger by roughly the spread-plus-adverse-selection cost that the simulation currently ignores.
